It's your last night in Barcelona and you want to take three friends somewhere good. That means phoning to book, explaining that one of you is vegetarian, sorting out a mistake on the bill, and deciding who pays. Lesson 2 got you through a café; this lesson brings everything since then into one evening, plus a trick for sounding polite that you can now understand: the imperfect from lesson 13.

**Warm-up (from lesson 17):** what does a waiter mean by **"Pasen, por favor"**? And what's the usted command of **decir**? The answers are at the end.

## Booking: the polite imperfect

To make a request sound softer, Spanish speakers often use the imperfect instead of the present. **Quería** literally means "I wanted", but here it works like English "I was wondering if…":

- **Quería reservar una mesa para cuatro para el sábado a las nueve.** I'd like to book a table for four for Saturday at nine.
- **¿Tienen algo a las nueve y media?** Do you have anything at half past nine?
- **A nombre de Nick.** Under the name Nick.
- **¿Tienen terraza?** Do you have outdoor seating?

Remember that dinner in Spain often starts at nine or later; at 7 pm many kitchens aren't open yet.

## At the table: special requests

- **Somos cuatro. Tenemos una reserva.** There are four of us. We have a reservation.
- **Mi amiga es vegetariana. ¿Qué platos no llevan carne?** My friend is vegetarian. Which dishes have no meat? **Llevar** is how Spanish says what's in a dish.
- **¿Lo pueden hacer sin gluten?** Can you make it gluten-free? (lesson 2's **sin**)
- **¿Qué lleva este plato?** What's in this dish?
- **Para compartir** to share. Ordering several plates for the table is normal, especially for tapas.
- **¿Nos trae otra botella de agua?** Could you bring us another bottle of water?

## When something's wrong

Stay gentle and give a reason, as in lesson 12:

- **Perdone, esto no es lo que pedí.** Excuse me, this isn't what I ordered. (**pedí**, preterite of pedir, lesson 10)
- **Creo que hay un error en la cuenta.** I think there's a mistake on the bill.
- **Pedimos dos cervezas, no tres.** We ordered two beers, not three.

## Paying, and who pays

- **La cuenta, por favor.** (lesson 2)
- **¿Podemos pagar por separado?** Can we pay separately? Many places in Spain prefer one bill; splitting it evenly among yourselves is common, and called **pagar a escote** in Spain.
- **¿El servicio está incluido?** Is service included?
- **Invito yo.** / **Te invito.** It's on me. In much of the Spanish-speaking world, the person who does the inviting usually pays. If someone says **te invito** when asking you out, they're offering to pay.
- **La próxima vez invito yo.** Next time it's on me. The gracious reply when someone treats you.

## Try it today

- **Restaurante:** Restaurante Can Solé, dígame.
- **Tú:** Hola, buenas tardes. Quería reservar una mesa para cuatro para esta noche.
- **Restaurante:** ¿A qué hora?
- **Tú:** A las nueve, si puede ser.
- **Restaurante:** A las nueve está completo. ¿Le va bien a las nueve y media?
- **Tú:** Perfecto. A nombre de Nick. Ah, y una persona es vegetariana.
- **Restaurante:** No hay problema. Hasta luego.

**Si puede ser** (if possible) is another softener worth stealing.

**Warm-up answers:** **Come in, please** (said to a group, ustedes), and **diga**.

## Problem

The bill arrives with three glasses of wine when your table ordered two, and you'd like to pay separately. Write what you'd say to the waiter: get their attention politely, explain the mistake, and ask to pay separately by card.

## Solution

One version:

> Perdone. Creo que hay un error en la cuenta: pedimos dos copas de vino, no tres. Y, ¿podemos pagar por separado, con tarjeta?

What to check in yours:

- **Perdone** to get attention, and **creo que** to soften the complaint.
- **Pedimos**: preterite of pedir, "we ordered" (looks like the present, as in lesson 10).
- **Copa** for a glass of wine; **vaso** is a tumbler for water.
- **Por separado** for separate payment, and **con tarjeta** for card.
