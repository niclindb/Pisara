You're at a bus station in Guadalajara. The departures board is in Spanish, the ticket window has a queue, and the bus to Tequila leaves in twenty minutes. You need four things fast: a ticket, the departure time, the platform, and confirmation that you're on the right bus. This lesson gives you each one as a single sentence.

**Warm-up (from lesson 13):** say "It was raining" and "When I was a child, I lived in Ohio". The answers are at the end.

## Buying a ticket

The word for ticket changes across the Atlantic: **un billete** in Spain, **un boleto** in most of Latin America. Both are understood everywhere.

- **Un billete / boleto para Sevilla, por favor.** A ticket to Seville, please.
- **De ida** one way, **de ida y vuelta** return (round trip).
- **¿Cuánto cuesta un billete de ida y vuelta?** How much is a return ticket? (lesson 4)
- **¿Hay descuento para…?** Is there a discount for…? Useful for students, seniors, or buying in advance.

## When and where

- **¿A qué hora sale el próximo tren a Toledo?** What time does the next train to Toledo leave? **Salir** is "to leave"; **llegar** is "to arrive".
- **¿A qué hora llega a Toledo?** What time does it get to Toledo?
- **¿De qué andén sale?** Which platform does it leave from? Bus stations say **dársena** or **puerta** for the bay or gate.
- **¿Cuánto tarda?** How long does it take? Answer: **Tarda dos horas.**
- **¿Hay que cambiar?** Do I have to change? **Hay que** + verb means "you have to", in general.

## On board

- **¿Este autobús va al centro?** Does this bus go downtown? Ask the driver before you get on.
- **¿Me avisa cuando lleguemos a la plaza, por favor?** Will you tell me when we get to the square? **Lleguemos** is a form you haven't met yet; learn the whole sentence as a phrase.
- **¿Está libre este asiento?** Is this seat free?
- **La próxima parada** the next stop. **Me bajo aquí.** I'm getting off here.

## Taxis and ride shares

- **¿Me lleva al aeropuerto, por favor?** Can you take me to the airport?
- **¿Cuánto cuesta más o menos?** Roughly how much will it be? Ask before you set off if there's no meter.
- **Aquí está bien, gracias.** Here's fine, thanks. The way to say "drop me here".

## How you travel: en, a pie

Spanish uses **en** for vehicles: **voy en tren**, **en autobús**, **en metro**, **en taxi**. On foot is the exception: **voy a pie** or **voy andando / caminando** (lesson 3).

## One word to avoid in Latin America

In Spain, **coger** is the everyday verb for "to catch": **coger el autobús**. In Mexico, Argentina and much of Latin America it's a vulgar word for sex. Use **tomar** instead: **tomar el autobús** is correct everywhere.

## Try it today

- **Tú:** Buenos días. Un boleto de ida y vuelta para Tequila, por favor.
- **Taquillera:** Son trescientos pesos. Sale a las diez y media.
- **Tú:** ¿De qué puerta sale?
- **Taquillera:** De la puerta cuatro.
- **Tú:** ¿Y cuánto tarda?
- **Taquillera:** Una hora y media, más o menos.

**Warm-up answers:** **Llovía** and **Cuando era niño, vivía en Ohio**.

## How do you ask "What time does the next bus leave?"

- ¿A qué hora va el próximo autobús?
- [x] ¿A qué hora sale el próximo autobús?
- ¿A qué hora llega el próximo autobús?

Salir is to leave or depart; llegar is to arrive.

## You're in Mexico City and want to say "I'm taking the metro." Which is safest?

- Cojo el metro
- [x] Tomo el metro
- Llevo el metro

Tomar works everywhere. Coger is fine in Spain but vulgar in much of Latin America.

## How do you say "We're going by train"?

- Vamos por tren
- Vamos con tren
- [x] Vamos en tren

Vehicles take en: en tren, en autobús, en taxi. On foot is a pie.

## Problem

You're at the train station in Madrid and want to go to Segovia today and come back tonight. At the ticket window: ask for the right ticket, ask what time the next train leaves and which platform it leaves from, and ask how long the trip takes.

## Solution

One version:

> Buenos días. Un billete de ida y vuelta para Segovia, por favor. ¿A qué hora sale el próximo tren? ¿De qué andén sale? ¿Y cuánto tarda?

What to check in yours:

- **Billete** in Spain (boleto would still be understood).
- **De ida y vuelta**, because you're coming back.
- **Sale** for when it leaves; **¿A qué hora llega?** if you also wanted the arrival time.
- **¿Cuánto tarda?** for how long. **¿Cuánto cuesta?** would ask the price instead.
