At a market stall in Oaxaca you find a hand-woven bag. You want to ask the price, see it in another color, ask for a better deal, and then say "I'll take it." That last phrase, **me lo llevo**, uses a small word you've seen in passing since lesson 3 (**¿Me lo puede mostrar?**) but never studied: **lo**, a pronoun that stands in for the thing you're talking about. This lesson teaches it with the shopping phrases that need it.

**Warm-up (from lesson 15):** ask "Can I pay by card?" (is it allowed?) and say "You have to pay the tourist tax". The answers are at the end.

## Asking and looking

- **¿Cuánto cuesta este bolso?** How much is this bag? (**este** = this one near me; **ese** = that one near you.)
- **¿Lo tiene en otro color?** Do you have it in another color? **¿En azul?** In blue?
- **¿Me lo puedo probar?** Can I try it on? The fitting room is **el probador**.
- **¿Qué talla usa?** What size are you? (clothes). For shoes, **¿Qué número calza?**
- **Solo estoy mirando, gracias.** I'm just looking, thanks.

## Lo, la, los, las: "it" and "them"

Instead of repeating the thing, Spanish replaces it with a pronoun that matches it:

| the thing | pronoun | example |
|---|---|---|
| **el bolso** (masculine) | **lo** | **Me lo llevo.** I'll take it. |
| **la camisa** (feminine) | **la** | **Me la llevo.** |
| **los zapatos** (plural) | **los** | **Me los llevo.** |
| **las gafas** (feminine plural) | **las** | **Me las llevo.** |

Two rules about where it goes:

- **Before a conjugated verb:** **Lo quiero.** I want it. **No la necesito.** I don't need it.
- **Or stuck to the end of an infinitive:** **¿Puedo probarlo?** is the same as **¿Lo puedo probar?** Pick whichever you remember.

In **me lo llevo**, **me** means "for myself", and it always comes first: **me lo**, never *lo me*.

## Bargaining

At markets and street stalls in much of Latin America, a little haggling is normal and friendly. In shops with marked prices, and in most of Spain, it isn't expected.

- **¿Me hace un descuento?** Can you give me a discount?
- **¿Me lo deja en doscientos?** Would you let me have it for two hundred?
- **Es un poco caro para mí.** It's a bit expensive for me.
- **Si me llevo dos, ¿cuánto es?** If I take two, how much is it?
- **Bueno, me lo llevo.** OK, I'll take it. Or **Lo voy a pensar, gracias.** I'll think about it, thanks: the polite way to walk away.

## Paying

- **¿Aceptan tarjeta?** Do you take cards? Small stalls often want **efectivo** (cash).
- **¿Me da una bolsa?** Can I have a bag?
- **¿Es para regalo?** Is it a gift? The shop may offer to wrap it.

## Try it today

- **Tú:** Buenas tardes. ¿Cuánto cuesta este bolso?
- **Vendedora:** Cuatrocientos pesos.
- **Tú:** Es muy bonito. ¿Lo tiene en verde?
- **Vendedora:** Sí, aquí está.
- **Tú:** Es un poco caro para mí. ¿Me lo deja en trescientos?
- **Vendedora:** Trescientos cincuenta, y es suyo.
- **Tú:** Bueno, me lo llevo.

**Es suyo** means "it's yours".

**Warm-up answers:** **¿Se puede pagar con tarjeta?** and **Hay que pagar la tasa turística**.

- [x] ¿Puedo probármela?
- ¿Puedo probármelo?
- ¿La me puedo probar?

Camisa is feminine, so la. It can attach to the infinitive (probármela) or go first as me la puedo probar, with me always before la.

## Problem

At a market in Cusco you like a scarf (**la bufanda**) priced at 80 soles. Ask if they have it in red, say it's a bit expensive and offer 60, then accept their counter-offer of 70 and ask if they take cards.

## Solution

One version:

> ¿La tiene en rojo? Es muy bonita, pero es un poco cara para mí. ¿Me la deja en sesenta? … Bueno, setenta. Me la llevo. ¿Aceptan tarjeta?

What to check in yours:

- **La** every time, because **bufanda** is feminine: la tiene, me la deja, me la llevo.
- **Bonita**, **cara**: the adjectives match the scarf too.
- **Me** before **la**, never after.
- If they say **solo efectivo**, it's cash only.
