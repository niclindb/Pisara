Asking the way is easy: one question does most of the work. The hard part is understanding the answer, so this lesson spends most of its time on what you'll hear back.

## Asking

Start with **Perdone** (excuse me, to a stranger) or **Disculpe**, then:

- **¿Dónde está el museo?** Where is the museum?
- **¿Dónde está la estación de metro más cercana?** Where's the nearest metro station?
- **¿Hay un baño por aquí?** Is there a bathroom around here? (**Hay**, pronounced like "eye", means "is there" or "there is".)
- **¿Cómo llego a la playa?** How do I get to the beach?
- **¿Está lejos?** Is it far? / **¿Está cerca?** Is it close?

**Dónde está** asks where something specific is (the museum). **Hay** asks whether one exists at all (a bathroom, a pharmacy, an ATM).

## Places you'll ask for

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| **el baño** | the bathroom (also **los servicios** in Spain) |
| **la farmacia** | the pharmacy |
| **un cajero** | an ATM |
| **la estación** | the station |
| **la parada de autobús** | the bus stop |
| **el hotel** | the hotel (the *h* is silent: oh-TEHL) |

## Understanding the answer

Listen for these words, and ignore the rest:

- **a la derecha**: to the right
- **a la izquierda**: to the left (eeth-KYEHR-dah in Spain, ees-KYEHR-dah in Latin America)
- **todo recto** or **derecho**: straight ahead. Careful: **derecho** (straight) and **derecha** (right) differ by one letter.
- **gire / tome**: turn / take ("gire a la izquierda", "tome la segunda calle")
- **la primera, la segunda, la tercera calle**: the first, second, third street
- **en la esquina**: on the corner
- **al final de la calle**: at the end of the street
- **a dos cuadras / a dos manzanas**: two blocks away (cuadras in Latin America, manzanas in Spain)
- **a cinco minutos andando / caminando**: five minutes on foot

## Two phrases that save you

- **¿Puede repetirlo más despacio, por favor?** Can you repeat that more slowly, please?
- **¿Me lo puede mostrar en el mapa?** Can you show me on the map? Hold up your phone. This one works even when you understand nothing else.

## Try it today

Read the answer below and trace the route in your head:

- **Tú:** Perdone, ¿dónde está la farmacia?
- **Señora:** Siga todo recto, y tome la segunda calle a la derecha. La farmacia está en la esquina.
- **Tú:** ¿Está lejos?
- **Señora:** No, a cinco minutos andando.
- **Tú:** Muchas gracias.

**Siga** means "keep going". Route: straight on, second street on the right, pharmacy on the corner, five minutes.

## Problem

You're looking for the bus stop. A man answers: "Está a tres cuadras. Siga derecho, y en la esquina del banco, gire a la izquierda. La parada está al final de la calle."

Write down the route in English, then write what you'd say to ask him whether it's far.

## Solution

**Route:** it's three blocks away. Keep going straight, turn left at the corner with the bank, and the stop is at the end of that street.

**To ask whether it's far:** "¿Está lejos?" or, more fully, "Perdone, ¿está lejos la parada?"

What to notice:

- **Cuadras** tells you he's likely in Latin America; in Spain he'd say **manzanas**.
- **Derecho** here means straight on, not right. The giveaway is "siga" (keep going): you keep going straight, not "keep going right".
- **En la esquina del banco**: "del" is "de + el", so it's "the corner of the bank".
