The first thirty seconds of any conversation use the same few phrases. Learn these and you can open and close almost any exchange in Spanish today.

## Saying hello

| Spanish | Sounds like | Use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Hola** | OH-lah | Hi, any time, anyone |
| **Buenos días** | BWEH-nohs DEE-ahs | Good morning, until about lunch |
| **Buenas tardes** | BWEH-nahs TAR-dehs | Good afternoon, lunch until dark |
| **Buenas noches** | BWEH-nahs NOH-chehs | Good evening, and also good night |

The *h* in **hola** is silent. A friendly habit: say **hola** plus the time of day, as in "Hola, buenos días," when you walk into a shop.

## Asking how someone is

- **¿Qué tal?** (keh TAHL): How's it going? Casual and safe almost anywhere.
- **¿Cómo estás?** How are you? (to a friend)
- **¿Cómo está?** How are you? (to a stranger or someone older)

Answer with **Bien, gracias. ¿Y tú?** (Fine, thanks. And you?) or **Muy bien** (very well) or **Más o menos** (so-so).

## Tú or usted?

Spanish has two words for "you". **Tú** is for friends, children and people your age in casual settings. **Usted** is for strangers, older people and anyone you'd call "sir" or "madam". With usted, the verb drops the *s*: **¿Cómo estás?** becomes **¿Cómo está?**

When in doubt, start with usted. People will tell you **"Puedes tutearme"** (you can use tú with me) if they prefer. In Spain, tú is common even with strangers; in much of Latin America, usted lasts longer.

## Saying who you are

- **Me llamo Nick.** My name is Nick. (Literally "I call myself Nick.") The *ll* sounds like the *y* in "yes".
- **Soy de Estados Unidos.** I'm from the United States. **Soy** means "I am" and works for your nationality, job and name: **Soy Nick. Soy ingeniero.**
- **¿Cómo te llamas?** What's your name? (tú) / **¿Cómo se llama?** (usted)
- **¿De dónde eres?** Where are you from? (tú) / **¿De dónde es?** (usted)
- **Mucho gusto.** Nice to meet you. A woman may also say **Encantada**, a man **Encantado**.

## Saying goodbye

- **Adiós**: goodbye
- **Hasta luego**: see you later. Used even when you won't, like "see you" in English.
- **Hasta mañana**: see you tomorrow
- **Chao**: bye, very common in Latin America

## Try it today

Say the whole exchange out loud twice:

- **A:** Hola, buenas tardes. ¿Cómo te llamas?
- **B:** Me llamo Ana. ¿Y tú?
- **A:** Me llamo Nick. Mucho gusto.
- **B:** Igualmente. ¿De dónde eres?
- **A:** Soy de Estados Unidos.

**Igualmente** means "likewise", the natural reply to mucho gusto.

## It's 7 pm and you walk into a small shop. What's a natural greeting?

- Buenos días
- [x] Hola, buenas noches
- Hasta luego

After dark, buenas noches works as "good evening", and adding hola makes it friendly.

## You're meeting your partner's grandmother for the first time. Which question fits best?

- ¿Cómo estás?
- [x] ¿Cómo está?
- ¿Qué tal, tío?

Usted (¿Cómo está?) shows respect to an older person you've just met.

## Someone says "Mucho gusto." What's a good reply?

- Adiós
- Me llamo
- [x] Igualmente

Igualmente means "likewise".

## Problem

Write out, in Spanish, what you would say to introduce yourself to a hotel receptionist in the afternoon: greet them, give your name, say where you're from, and say it's nice to meet them.

## Solution

One natural version:

> Hola, buenas tardes. Me llamo Nick. Soy de Estados Unidos. Mucho gusto.

Things to check in your answer:

- **Buenas tardes**, because it's afternoon.
- If you asked them anything, use **usted**: "¿Cómo está?" rather than "¿Cómo estás?", since a receptionist is a stranger in a formal setting.
- **Soy de** plus the place, not "Estoy de".

If they answer "Bienvenido" (welcome), a simple **Gracias** is perfect.
