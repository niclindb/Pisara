You've met someone friendly at a language exchange, and they say **"¿Quedamos el sábado?"** That's an invitation, and in a few seconds you need to say yes, suggest a time and a place, or turn it down without sounding rude. This lesson puts lessons 4, 7 and 8 to work: times, the present tense and **querer**, **poder** and **tener que**.

**Warm-up (from lesson 8):** say "I can't" and "I have to work". The answers are at the end.

## Days and when

| | |
|---|---|
| **lunes** Monday | **viernes** Friday |
| **martes** Tuesday | **sábado** Saturday |
| **miércoles** Wednesday | **domingo** Sunday |
| **jueves** Thursday | |

- Days are lowercase in Spanish.
- **el sábado** means "on Saturday", with no word for "on". **Los sábados** means "on Saturdays", every week.
- **hoy** today, **mañana** tomorrow, **pasado mañana** the day after tomorrow, **esta noche** tonight, **este fin de semana** this weekend, **la semana que viene** next week.

## Inviting

- **¿Quieres** + verb? Do you want to…? **¿Quieres tomar un café?**
- **¿Te apetece** + verb or noun? Do you fancy…? Very common in Spain; **¿Te gustaría…?** (Would you like…?) works everywhere.
- **¿Quedamos?** Shall we meet up? **Quedar** means to arrange to meet: **He quedado con Ana** is "I've arranged to meet Ana". It's used across the Spanish-speaking world, most of all in Spain.
- **¿Por qué no vamos a…?** Why don't we go to…?

## Settling the details

- **¿A qué hora quedamos?** What time shall we meet?
- **¿Dónde quedamos?** Where shall we meet?
- **¿Te va bien a las siete?** Does seven work for you? (Spain) / **¿Te queda bien a las siete?** (more common in Latin America)
- **Quedamos en la puerta del museo.** Let's meet at the museum entrance. **Quedar en** + place.
- **Nos vemos el sábado.** See you on Saturday.

## Saying yes, no, or maybe

- **¡Claro! / ¡Vale! / ¡Dale!** Sure! (Dale in much of Latin America.)
- **Me encantaría.** I'd love to.
- **Lo siento, no puedo. Tengo que trabajar.** Sorry, I can't. I have to work. Giving a reason with **tengo que** is the polite way to decline.
- **¿Y si quedamos el domingo?** What if we meet on Sunday instead?
- **Te digo algo mañana.** I'll let you know tomorrow.

Notice that the plans themselves use the plain present (lesson 7): **¿Quedamos el sábado?** literally asks "Do we meet on Saturday?" For a fixed arrangement, Spanish uses the present where English says "Shall we…?" or "Are we…?"

## Try it today

- **Lucía:** ¿Te apetece ir al cine este fin de semana?
- **Tú:** ¡Me encantaría! Pero el sábado no puedo, tengo que trabajar. ¿Y si vamos el domingo?
- **Lucía:** Vale. ¿Te va bien a las seis?
- **Tú:** Perfecto. ¿Dónde quedamos?
- **Lucía:** En la puerta del cine. ¡Nos vemos el domingo!

**Warm-up answers:** **No puedo** and **Tengo que trabajar**.

Go deeper: SpanishDict's entry for [quedar](https://www.spanishdict.com/translate/quedar) shows its many meanings (to meet, to stay, to be left, to fit) with example sentences.

## How do you say "on Mondays" (every week)?

- [x] los lunes
- en lunes
- el lunes

Los + day means every week; el + day means one particular day.

## A friend asks "¿Quedamos a las ocho?" You can't make it because you have to work. What's best?

- No quiero, tengo trabajo
- [x] No puedo, tengo que trabajar
- No me gusta a las ocho

No puedo plus a reason with tener que is a polite refusal. No quiero sounds like you don't want to see them.

## How do you say "Let's meet at the station"?

- Quedamos a la estación
- [x] Quedamos en la estación
- Quedamos con la estación

Quedar en + a place says where; quedar con + a person says who.

## Problem

A colleague messages: "¿Te apetece cenar el viernes?" You can't on Friday because you're going to a concert. Write a reply that declines politely with a reason, suggests another day and a time, and asks where to meet.

## Solution

One version:

> ¡Me encantaría! Pero el viernes no puedo, voy a un concierto. ¿Y si cenamos el sábado? ¿Te va bien a las nueve? ¿Dónde quedamos?

What to check in yours:

- **Start warm** (me encantaría, ¡qué buena idea!) before the **no puedo**.
- **Give a reason**: voy a un concierto, tengo que trabajar. **Voy a** + place uses **ir** (lesson 8).
- **El sábado** without a word for "on".
- **¿Dónde quedamos?** or **¿Quedamos en…?** to settle the place.
