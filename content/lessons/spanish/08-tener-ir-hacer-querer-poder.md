Listen to a day of real Spanish and a handful of verbs carry most of it: having, going, doing, wanting and being able to. They're also the ones that break the pattern from lesson 7. That's no coincidence: the verbs people use most are the ones that wear down and change over centuries. Learn these five and you can ask for nearly anything.

**Warm-up (from lesson 7):** say "We eat at nine" and "Do you speak English?" (to a friend). The answers are at the end.

## The five

| | **tener** (have) | **ir** (go) | **hacer** (do, make) | **querer** (want) | **poder** (can) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| yo | **tengo** | **voy** | **hago** | **quiero** | **puedo** |
| tú | tienes | vas | haces | quieres | puedes |
| él, ella, usted | tiene | va | hace | quiere | puede |
| nosotros | tenemos | vamos | hacemos | queremos | podemos |
| ellos, ustedes | tienen | van | hacen | quieren | pueden |

You already know **voy** (lesson 5's **voy a**) and **hace** (lesson 5's **hace calor**). The other forms are the same verbs.

## Two patterns hide in that table

**The -go verbs.** A few common verbs add a **g** in the yo form only: **tengo**, **hago**, and also **salgo** (I go out), **pongo** (I put), **vengo** (I come), **digo** (I say). The rest of each verb is close to regular.

**The boot verbs.** In **quiero** and **puedo**, the vowel in the middle of the verb changes, but not in the "we" form: **queremos**, **podemos**. Draw a line around the forms that change in the table and it makes the shape of a boot, which is why teachers call them boot verbs. The common changes:

- **e → ie**: querer (quiero), preferir (prefiero), cerrar (cierro), empezar (empiezo)
- **o → ue**: poder (puedo), volver (vuelvo, I come back), dormir (duermo), costar (cuesta, from lesson 4's **¿cuánto cuesta?**)
- **e → i**: pedir (pido, I order or ask for), repetir (repito)

Tener and venir do both: **tengo** but **tienes**.

## What they unlock

Each verb opens a set of everyday phrases. These are the ones to steal:

- **Tengo que** + verb: I have to. **Tengo que trabajar.** I have to work.
- **Tener** for things English says with "be": **Tengo hambre** (I'm hungry), **tengo sed** (thirsty), **tengo frío** (cold), **tengo 30 años** (I'm 30).
- **Quiero** + noun or verb: **Quiero un taxi. Quiero ver la catedral.** It's direct; with strangers, lesson 2's **quisiera** sounds softer.
- **¿Puedo** + verb?: may I? **¿Puedo pagar con tarjeta?** (also from lesson 2)
- **¿Puede** + verb?: can you? **¿Puede ayudarme?** Can you help me? This turns any verb into a polite request.
- **¿Qué haces?** What are you doing? / What do you do? **¿Qué vas a hacer hoy?** What are you going to do today?

## Try it today

- **A:** Tengo hambre. ¿Quieres comer algo?
- **B:** Sí, pero no puedo tardar. Tengo que volver al trabajo a las tres.
- **A:** Vale. Hay un bar aquí cerca. Yo pido una tortilla. ¿Y tú?
- **B:** Prefiero algo ligero. ¿Puede traernos la carta, por favor?

**Vale** (OK) is everywhere in Spain; in Latin America you'll hear **está bien** or **dale**.

**Warm-up answers:** **Comemos a las nueve** and **¿Hablas inglés?**

## How do you say "I'm hungry"?

- Estoy hambre
- Soy hambriento
- [x] Tengo hambre

Hunger, thirst, cold and age all use tener: you "have" hunger.

## How do you say "We can come back tomorrow"?

- Puedemos volver mañana
- [x] Podemos volver mañana
- Podimos volver mañana

The "we" form sits outside the boot, so the o doesn't change to ue.

## Which sentence means "I have to leave at eight"?

- [x] Tengo que salir a las ocho
- Tengo de salir a las ocho
- Tengo a salir a las ocho

Tener que + verb means "have to". Salir is a -go verb: salgo, but the infinitive stays salir.

## Problem

You're at a hotel reception in Lima. In four sentences, using a different verb from this lesson in each one: say you have a reservation, ask if you can leave your bags, say you want to see the old town today, and ask what time breakfast starts.

## Solution

One version:

> Buenas tardes. Tengo una reserva a nombre de Lindberg. ¿Puedo dejar las maletas aquí? Quiero ver el centro histórico hoy. ¿A qué hora empieza el desayuno?

What to check in yours:

- **Tengo** una reserva: the yo form of tener has the g.
- **¿Puedo** + verb asks permission; **¿Puede** + verb would ask the receptionist to do it.
- **Quiero** + infinitive: the second verb stays in its dictionary form (**ver**).
- **Empieza**: e → ie, because it's inside the boot. If you said **¿A qué hora es el desayuno?**, that's right too: time uses ser (lesson 6).
