A security officer at the airport in Bogotá says **"Ponga el teléfono en la bandeja y pase, por favor."** A sign on the door says **"Empuje"**. A pharmacist tells you **"Tome una pastilla cada ocho horas."** Every one of these is a command, and you met three of them back in lesson 3: **siga**, **gire**, **tome**. As a traveler you'll hear commands far more than you give them, so this lesson is about recognizing them instantly.

**Warm-up (from lesson 16):** say "I'll take it" about a shirt (**la camisa**), and "Do you have it in blue?" about a bag (**el bolso**). The answers are at the end.

## How a polite command is built

The polite (usted) command is built from the yo form of the present (lessons 7 and 8):

1. Take the yo form: **tomo**, **sigo**, **pongo**.
2. Drop the **-o**: tom-, sig-, pong-.
3. Add the "opposite" vowel: **-e** for -ar verbs, **-a** for -er and -ir verbs.

| verb | yo | command |
|---|---|---|
| **tomar** (take) | tomo | **tome** |
| **girar** (turn) | giro | **gire** |
| **seguir** (keep going) | sigo | **siga** |
| **poner** (put) | pongo | **ponga** |
| **hacer** (do) | hago | **haga** |
| **venir** (come) | vengo | **venga** |
| **decir** (say) | digo | **diga** |

Because the command starts from the yo form, everything irregular in lesson 8 carries over: the -go verbs give **ponga**, **haga**, **venga**, **diga**, and the boot verbs give **cierre** (close), **vuelva** (come back), **pida** (order).

Addressing a group (ustedes), add **-n**: **tomen**, **sigan**, **pasen**.

## Three that don't follow the rule

- **ir** → **vaya** (go): **Vaya a la puerta doce.** Go to gate twelve.
- **ser** → **sea**: **Sea puntual.** Be on time.
- **estar** → **esté**: **Esté aquí a las ocho.** Be here at eight.

## Pronouns stick to the end

When a command has a pronoun (lesson 16), it attaches to the end, and an accent keeps the stress where it was:

- **Dígame.** Tell me. (Many people answer the phone this way in Spain.)
- **Siéntese, por favor.** Sit down, please.
- **Pruébelo.** Try it.

## Signs and recipes use the infinitive

Written instructions often skip the command form and use the plain infinitive: **No fumar** (no smoking), **No tocar** (don't touch), **Mantener la derecha** (keep right). On doors, Spain says **Empuje / Tire** (push / pull); in Latin America you'll often see **Jale** or **Hale** for pull.

## Commands you'll hear on a trip

- **Pase, por favor.** Come in / go ahead.
- **Espere aquí.** Wait here.
- **Firme aquí.** Sign here.
- **Ponga sus cosas en la bandeja.** Put your things in the tray.
- **Tome una pastilla cada ocho horas.** Take one pill every eight hours.
- **Siga recto y gire a la izquierda.** Go straight on and turn left.
- **Bájese en la próxima parada.** Get off at the next stop.

## Try it today

- **Agente:** Buenos días. Su pasaporte, por favor.
- **Tú:** Aquí tiene.
- **Agente:** Ponga el dedo aquí. Ahora mire a la cámara.
- **Agente:** Muy bien. Pase.

**Aquí tiene** (here you are) is the polite way to hand something over.

**Warm-up answers:** **Me la llevo** and **¿Lo tiene en azul?**

- Hace
- Hacé
- [x] Haga

Start from the yo form hago, drop the -o and add -a: haga.

## A door says "Tire". In Spain, what do you do?

- [x] Pull the door
- Push the door
- Knock first

In Spain, tire means pull and empuje means push.

## Problem

A pharmacist in Santiago gives you this advice: "Tome una pastilla cada doce horas, con comida. No beba alcohol. Si no está mejor en tres días, vaya al médico." Write out what you've been told to do in English, and give the infinitive (dictionary form) of each command.

## Solution

What you've been told:

1. Take one pill every twelve hours, with food. (**tome** → tomar)
2. Don't drink alcohol. (**beba** → beber)
3. If you're not better in three days, go to the doctor. (**vaya** → ir)

What to check in yours:

- **Beba** has the -a ending because beber is an -er verb.
- **Vaya** is one of the three irregulars; it doesn't come from a yo form.
- **Está mejor** is not a command, it's estar for a condition (lesson 6).
- A negative command just adds **no** in front: **no beba**.
