So far you've borrowed whole phrases: **me gusta**, **voy a**, **¿dónde está?** To say something new, like "I work from home" or "We live near the beach", you need to build a verb yourself. The good news is that most Spanish verbs follow one of three patterns, and once you know them you can use hundreds of verbs you've never practised.

**Warm-up (from lesson 6):** "I'm tired" and "I'm a programmer". Ser or estar? The answers are at the end.

## Three families

Every Spanish verb in the dictionary ends in **-ar**, **-er** or **-ir**: **hablar** (to speak), **comer** (to eat), **vivir** (to live). To use one, drop those two letters and add an ending that says *who* is doing it.

| | **hablar** | **comer** | **vivir** |
|---|---|---|---|
| yo | habl**o** | com**o** | viv**o** |
| tú | habl**as** | com**es** | viv**es** |
| él, ella, usted | habl**a** | com**e** | viv**e** |
| nosotros | habl**amos** | com**emos** | viv**imos** |
| ellos, ustedes | habl**an** | com**en** | viv**en** |

Two shortcuts make this lighter than it looks:

- **Yo always ends in -o.** Hablo, como, vivo, trabajo, escribo.
- **-er and -ir are identical** except for "we": com**emos** but viv**imos**.

(In Spain, vosotros adds **habláis**, **coméis**, **vivís**.)

## You can drop the "I"

The ending already says who's acting, so Spanish usually leaves out **yo**, **tú** and **nosotros**. **Hablo inglés** is the normal way to say "I speak English". **Yo hablo inglés** adds emphasis, like "*I* speak English (and she doesn't)".

## Negatives and questions

- **No** goes right before the verb: **No hablo francés.** I don't speak French. There's no "do" in Spanish.
- A question is the same sentence with a rising voice: **¿Hablas inglés?** Do you speak English? In writing, Spanish opens it with **¿**.

## The present covers more than English's

One form does the work of three English ones. **Trabajo en casa** can mean "I work at home", "I'm working at home" or "I do work at home". It also covers the near future: **Mañana como con Ana.** I'm having lunch with Ana tomorrow.

## Twelve verbs worth knowing

| -ar | -er | -ir |
|---|---|---|
| **trabajar** to work | **comer** to eat | **vivir** to live |
| **necesitar** to need | **beber** to drink | **escribir** to write |
| **buscar** to look for | **aprender** to learn | **abrir** to open |
| **llegar** to arrive | **leer** to read | **compartir** to share |

## Try it today

- **A:** ¿Dónde vives?
- **B:** Vivo en Chicago, pero trabajo en casa. ¿Y tú?
- **A:** Vivo aquí, en Madrid. ¿Hablas español en el trabajo?
- **B:** No, hablo inglés. Pero aprendo español todos los días.
- **A:** ¡Qué bien! ¿A qué hora abren las tiendas?
- **B:** Abren a las diez.

**Warm-up answers:** **Estoy cansado** (a condition, estar) and **Soy programador** (an occupation, ser).

Go deeper: SpanishDict, [-AR verbs](https://www.spanishdict.com/guide/-ar-verbs), which walks through the pattern with more examples.

## How do you say "We live in Texas"?

- Vivemos en Texas
- [x] Vivimos en Texas
- Vivamos en Texas

-ir verbs keep the i in the "we" form: vivimos.

## How do you say "I don't need a bag"?

- [x] No necesito una bolsa
- Necesito no una bolsa
- Yo no necesita bolsa

No goes directly before the verb, and the yo form ends in -o.

## The museum is closing. Which sentence says "They're closing at six"?

- Cierran a las seis horas
- [x] Cierran a las seis
- Son cierran a las seis

The plain present covers "they're closing", and times take "a las" (lesson 4). Cerrar is one of the verbs in lesson 8 that changes its stem: cierran, not cerran.

## Problem

A new colleague in Mexico City messages you: "¿Dónde vives? ¿Hablas español?" Reply in four sentences: where you live, where you work, which languages you speak, and one thing you're learning or reading. Use at least one -ar, one -er and one -ir verb.

## Solution

One version:

> Vivo en Chicago. Trabajo en casa, para una empresa de tecnología. Hablo inglés y un poco de español. Ahora leo un libro sobre diseño de sistemas.

What to check in yours:

- Every "I" verb ends in **-o**: vivo, trabajo, hablo, leo.
- You didn't need **yo** in front of any of them.
- **Un poco de español**: "a little Spanish" needs **de**.
- If you said what you're doing at the moment, the plain present (**leo**) is the natural choice. **Estoy leyendo** is also correct, but Spanish uses it less than English uses "I'm reading".
