Numbers come up all day: prices, times, platforms, room numbers. You only need to memorize about thirty words to say any number up to 100.

## 0 to 15: learn these by heart

| 0 to 3 | 4 to 7 | 8 to 11 | 12 to 15 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 **cero** | 4 **cuatro** | 8 **ocho** | 12 **doce** |
| 1 **uno** | 5 **cinco** | 9 **nueve** | 13 **trece** |
| 2 **dos** | 6 **seis** | 10 **diez** | 14 **catorce** |
| 3 **tres** | 7 **siete** | 11 **once** | 15 **quince** |

Before a noun, **uno** shortens to **un** or **una**: un café, una cerveza.

## 16 to 99: a pattern

From 16 to 29, the number is one word: **dieciséis** (16), **diecisiete**, **dieciocho**, **diecinueve**, **veinte** (20), **veintiuno**, **veintidós**…

From 30 on, it's tens + **y** (and) + units:

| Tens | Example |
|---|---|
| 30 **treinta** | 35 **treinta y cinco** |
| 40 **cuarenta** | 48 **cuarenta y ocho** |
| 50 **cincuenta** | 56 **cincuenta y seis** |
| 60 **sesenta** | 61 **sesenta y uno** |
| 70 **setenta** | 72 **setenta y dos** |
| 80 **ochenta** | 83 **ochenta y tres** |
| 90 **noventa** | 99 **noventa y nueve** |

And 100 is **cien**. Watch **sesenta** (60) and **setenta** (70): they sound close.

## Prices

- **¿Cuánto cuesta?** How much does it cost? Pointing at the item is fine.
- **¿Cuánto es?** How much is it (all together)? Use it at the till.
- **Es muy caro.** It's very expensive. / **Es barato.** It's cheap.

Prices are read as euros (or pesos) **con** cents: €4.50 is **cuatro euros con cincuenta** or just **cuatro cincuenta**. Spain writes it 4,50 €, with a comma.

If you don't catch a price, ask **¿Me lo escribe?** (Could you write it down?), or look at the card reader.

## Telling the time

Ask **¿Qué hora es?** (What time is it?). The answer uses **es** for one o'clock and **son** for the rest:

- **Es la una.** It's one o'clock.
- **Son las tres.** It's three o'clock.
- **Son las tres y cuarto.** 3:15 (and a quarter)
- **Son las tres y media.** 3:30 (and a half)
- **Son las cuatro menos cuarto.** 3:45 (four minus a quarter). In Latin America you'll also hear **un cuarto para las cuatro**.
- **Son las seis y diez.** 6:10

To say *when* something happens, use **a las**: **El tren sale a las ocho.** The train leaves at eight. Add **de la mañana** (am), **de la tarde** (afternoon/early evening) or **de la noche** (night) if it's not obvious. Timetables use the 24-hour clock: **a las dieciocho treinta** is 6:30 pm.

## Try it today

Say every price and time you see today in Spanish in your head: the coffee (**tres cincuenta**), the clock on your screen (**son las cuatro y veinte**), the meeting (**a las diez**).

## How do you say 47?

- cuarenta siete
- [x] cuarenta y siete
- cuatro y siete

From 31 to 99: tens, then y, then units.

## The clock says 1:00. What's correct?

- Son las una
- [x] Es la una
- Es las uno

One o'clock is singular: es la una. Every other hour uses son las.

## Someone says the museum closes "a las seis menos cuarto." When should you be there by?

- 6:15
- [x] 5:45
- 6:45

Menos cuarto means a quarter before: six minus a quarter is 5:45.

## Problem

Write these in Spanish, as you'd say them out loud:

1. A ticket costs €23.80.
2. It's 9:30 in the morning.
3. The bus leaves at 4:45 pm.
4. Ask how much a bottle of water costs.

## Solution

1. **Veintitrés euros con ochenta** (or "veintitrés ochenta"). 21 to 29 are one word, and veintitrés has an accent.
2. **Son las nueve y media de la mañana.**
3. **El autobús sale a las cinco menos cuarto de la tarde.** On a timetable it would be **a las dieciséis cuarenta y cinco**. In Latin America: "a un cuarto para las cinco".
4. **¿Cuánto cuesta una botella de agua?**
