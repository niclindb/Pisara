It's 11 pm, the air conditioning in your hotel room has stopped, and you've just noticed your phone charger is still in the taxi. Travel problems are when Spanish matters most, and also when it's hardest to think. The fix is a short script you can lean on: say there's a problem, say what it is, and ask for help. You already have most of the grammar from lessons 6, 8 and 11.

**Warm-up (from lesson 11):** say "I went to the pharmacy" and "I had a problem". The answers are at the end.

## The three-step script

1. **Name it:** **Perdone, tengo un problema.** Excuse me, I have a problem.
2. **Say what:** one of the sentences below.
3. **Ask:** **¿Me puede ayudar?** Can you help me? or **¿Qué puedo hacer?** What can I do?

## Things that don't work

- **No funciona el aire acondicionado.** The air conditioning doesn't work. **Funcionar** is the verb for machines: **no funciona la ducha** (the shower), **la llave** (the key), **el wifi**.
- **No hay agua caliente.** There's no hot water. (**Hay**, from lesson 3.)
- **La habitación está sucia.** The room is dirty. A condition, so estar (lesson 6).
- **¿Puedo cambiar de habitación?** Can I change rooms?

## Things that are lost or taken

These are past events, so they use the preterite (lessons 10 and 11):

- **Perdí mi pasaporte.** I lost my passport.
- **Dejé mi cargador en el taxi.** I left my charger in the taxi.
- **Me robaron la cartera.** My wallet was stolen. (Literally "they stole the wallet from me".)
- **Perdí el tren.** I missed the train. **Perder** covers both losing and missing.

## When you feel unwell

To say something hurts, **doler** works like **gustar** from lesson 5: the thing that hurts is the subject, and **me** says who it hurts.

- **Me duele la cabeza.** My head hurts. **Me duelen los pies.** My feet hurt (plural, so duelen).
- **Me siento mal.** I feel ill. **Estoy mareado / mareada.** I'm dizzy.
- **Necesito un médico.** I need a doctor. **¿Hay una farmacia cerca?** Is there a pharmacy nearby?
- **Soy alérgico a la penicilina.** I'm allergic to penicillin (lesson 2's **alérgico a**).

In an emergency, the number in Spain and the rest of the EU is **112**; Mexico uses **911**. Other countries vary, so look it up when you land.

## Keeping it polite under pressure

Strangers help more when you start softly: **Perdone** or **Disculpe**, **por favor**, and a **muchas gracias** at the end. If they talk too fast, lesson 3's **¿Puede repetirlo más despacio?** still works.

## Try it today

- **Tú:** Buenas noches. Perdone, tengo un problema. No funciona el aire acondicionado en la habitación 214.
- **Recepcionista:** Lo siento mucho. Mañana viene el técnico.
- **Tú:** Es que hace mucho calor y no puedo dormir. ¿Puedo cambiar de habitación?
- **Recepcionista:** Sí, claro. Tenemos una libre en el tercer piso.
- **Tú:** Muchas gracias. Ah, y otra cosa: dejé mi cargador en el taxi. ¿Me puede ayudar a llamar a la compañía?

**Es que…** (it's just that…) softens a complaint by giving a reason. It's one of the most useful phrases in the language.

**Warm-up answers:** **Fui a la farmacia** and **Tuve un problema**.

## How do you say "My feet hurt"?

- Me duele los pies
- [x] Me duelen los pies
- Tengo dolor los pies

Doler works like gustar: the feet are the subject, and they're plural, so duelen.

## The shower in your room doesn't work. What do you say?

- La ducha no trabaja
- La ducha no está
- [x] La ducha no funciona

Machines and devices funcionan. Trabajar is for people doing a job.

## How do you say "I missed the bus"?

- [x] Perdí el autobús
- Falté el autobús
- Extrañé el autobús

Perder means both to lose and to miss a bus, train or flight.

## Problem

You're at a pharmacy in Buenos Aires. Since yesterday you've had a headache and a sore throat (**la garganta**), and you're allergic to aspirin (**la aspirina**). Explain this to the pharmacist and ask what they recommend.

## Solution

One version:

> Buenas tardes. Desde ayer me duele la cabeza y me duele la garganta. Soy alérgico a la aspirina. ¿Qué me recomienda?

What to check in yours:

- **Me duele** + a singular body part, and **me duelen** + plural.
- **La cabeza**, **la garganta**: Spanish uses **la**, not "my", for body parts, because **me** already says whose.
- **Soy alérgico / alérgica**: an allergy is part of who you are, so ser.
- **¿Qué me recomienda?** from lesson 2: usted, because the pharmacist is a stranger.
