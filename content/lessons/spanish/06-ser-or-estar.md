You've already used both Spanish verbs for "to be" without noticing. In lesson 1 you said **Soy de Estados Unidos**. In lesson 3 you asked **¿Dónde está el museo?** In lesson 5 you said **Estoy de vacaciones**. English has one verb here; Spanish has two, **ser** and **estar**, and picking the wrong one is the most common mistake beginners make. This lesson gives you a rule that gets it right nearly every time.

**Warm-up (from lesson 5):** before reading on, say "I like tacos" and "It's cold" in Spanish. The answers are at the end of the lesson.

## The forms

| | **ser** | **estar** |
|---|---|---|
| yo (I) | soy | estoy |
| tú (you) | eres | estás |
| él, ella, usted (he, she, you formal) | es | está |
| nosotros (we) | somos | estamos |
| ellos, ustedes (they, you all) | son | están |

(Spain also uses **vosotros** for "you all" among friends: **sois**, **estáis**. You'll hear it there, but **ustedes** is understood everywhere.)

## The rule: what it is, or how and where it is

**Ser** says what something *is*: its identity and the traits that define it. **Estar** says how something is *right now*, or where it is.

SpanishDict's [ser vs estar guide](https://www.spanishdict.com/guide/ser-vs-estar) packs this into two memory words:

- **ser = DOCTOR**: **D**escription, **O**ccupation, **C**haracteristic, **T**ime, **O**rigin, **R**elationship.
- **estar = PLACE**: **P**osition, **L**ocation, **A**ction (in progress), **C**ondition, **E**motion.

In practice:

- **Soy Nick. Soy ingeniero. Soy de Chicago.** Name, job, origin: ser.
- **Mi hermana es alta y simpática.** My sister is tall and nice. Description: ser.
- **Son las ocho. Hoy es lunes.** Time and day: ser (you met **son las…** in lesson 4).
- **Estoy cansado.** I'm tired. A condition that will pass: estar.
- **Estoy en el hotel. El banco está cerca.** Location: estar.
- **Estoy contento. Ella está nerviosa.** Emotions: estar.
- **Estoy comiendo.** I'm eating (right now). An action in progress: estar.

## Two traps

**Events use ser for their location.** A thing or person is *located* with estar, but an event *takes place*, so it uses ser: **La fiesta es en mi casa.** The party is at my house. **El concierto es en el parque.** Compare **Mi casa está en el centro.** My house is downtown.

**Some adjectives change meaning.** The verb tells you whether you mean the trait or the state:

| | with **ser** | with **estar** |
|---|---|---|
| **aburrido** | boring | bored |
| **listo** | clever | ready |
| **rico** | rich | tasty |
| **malo** | bad | sick |

So **¡Está muy rico!** about your dinner is a compliment to the cook, and **¿Estás listo?** means "Are you ready?", not "Are you clever?"

## A quick test when you're stuck

Ask: *could this be different in an hour, or is it about where something is?* If yes, estar. If it's about who or what something is, ser. Being dead is the famous exception (**está muerto**), but you won't need it at dinner.

## Try it today

- **A:** Hola, ¿qué tal? ¿Estás bien?
- **B:** Estoy un poco cansado. ¿Dónde estás?
- **A:** Estoy en el café de la plaza. Es muy bonito.
- **B:** ¿La cena es a las nueve?
- **A:** Sí, y es en casa de Ana. ¡La comida siempre está riquísima!

**Warm-up answers:** **Me gustan los tacos** (gustan, because tacos are plural) and **Hace frío**.

Go deeper: SpanishDict, [Ser vs. Estar](https://www.spanishdict.com/guide/ser-vs-estar), with a longer list of meaning-changing adjectives.

## How do you say "The museum is closed today"?

- El museo es cerrado hoy
- [x] El museo está cerrado hoy
- El museo hay cerrado hoy

Closed is a condition that changes (it'll open tomorrow), so estar.

## How do you say "The party is at Marta's house"?

- [x] La fiesta es en casa de Marta
- La fiesta está en casa de Marta
- La fiesta hay en casa de Marta

A party is an event, and an event's location uses ser.

## A friend tastes your paella and says "¡Está muy rica!" What do they mean?

- It's very expensive
- [x] It's really tasty
- It's very rich food

Rico with estar means tasty. With ser it means rich (wealthy).

## Problem

Write four short sentences about yourself right now: your name, your job, where you are, and how you feel. Then add one about a friend or family member: who they are to you and what they're like.

## Solution

One version:

> Soy Nick. Soy programador. Estoy en casa. Estoy un poco cansado. Mi hermana es médica y es muy divertida.

What to check in yours:

- **Name, job, relationships and personality** all take **ser**: soy, es.
- **Where you are** and **how you feel** take **estar**: estoy en casa, estoy cansado.
- No article before a job after ser: **soy programador**, not *soy un programador*.
- **Cansado** for a man, **cansada** for a woman; the adjective matches the person.
