It's Monday morning and a colleague asks **"¿Qué tal el fin de semana?"** Until now, every verb you've learned has been about the present or the future. To answer, you need the past. Spanish has two main past tenses, and this lesson covers the one for finished events: the **preterite** (*pretérito*). It's the "what happened" tense: I arrived, we ate, she called.

**Warm-up (from lesson 7):** give the yo and nosotros forms of **hablar** and **vivir** in the present. The answers are at the end.

## The endings

Drop the -ar, -er or -ir as before, and add these:

| | **hablar** | **comer** | **vivir** |
|---|---|---|---|
| yo | habl**é** | com**í** | viv**í** |
| tú | habl**aste** | com**iste** | viv**iste** |
| él, ella, usted | habl**ó** | com**ió** | viv**ió** |
| nosotros | habl**amos** | com**imos** | viv**imos** |
| ellos, ustedes | habl**aron** | com**ieron** | viv**ieron** |

(Spain's vosotros: **hablasteis**, **comisteis**, **vivisteis**.)

As in the present, **-er and -ir verbs share one set of endings**, so there are really only two patterns to learn.

## The accent matters

**Hablo** means "I speak". **Habló** means "he spoke". The only difference is where the stress falls, and the written accent marks it. Say them aloud: **HA-blo** versus **ha-BLO**. The same goes for **trabajo** (I work) and **trabajó** (she worked).

## Nosotros looks like the present

For -ar and -ir verbs, the "we" form is the same in both tenses: **hablamos** is "we speak" or "we spoke", **vivimos** "we live" or "we lived". The time words around it tell you which. -er verbs differ: **comemos** (we eat), **comimos** (we ate).

## A spelling change in the yo form

Verbs ending in **-car**, **-gar** and **-zar** change one letter in the yo form, so they keep the sound they have in the infinitive:

- **buscar** → **busqué** (I looked for)
- **llegar** → **llegué** (I arrived)
- **pagar** → **pagué** (I paid)
- **empezar** → **empecé** (I started)

Only the spelling changes. Every other form is regular: **llegaste**, **llegó**.

## Words that signal the preterite

- **ayer** yesterday, **anoche** last night, **anteayer** the day before yesterday
- **el lunes**, **el fin de semana**: on Monday, at the weekend (as in lesson 9, no word for "on")
- **la semana pasada**, **el año pasado**: last week, last year
- **hace dos días**: two days ago. **Hace** + time = "ago".

## Try it today

- **Marta:** ¿Qué tal el fin de semana?
- **Tú:** Muy bien. El sábado visité a mi hermana. Comimos en un restaurante peruano.
- **Marta:** ¡Qué rico! ¿Y el domingo?
- **Tú:** Descansé. Leí un poco y por la noche miré una película. ¿Y tú?
- **Marta:** Yo trabajé, pero anoche cené con unos amigos.

**Warm-up answers:** **hablo, hablamos** and **vivo, vivimos**. Notice those nosotros forms are also the preterite.

## How do you say "She arrived at nine"?

- Llego a las nueve
- Llegué a las nueve
- [x] Llegó a las nueve

Llegó, with the stress on the last syllable, is he/she/you formal. Llego is "I arrive" and llegué is "I arrived".

## How do you say "I paid the bill"?

- Pagé la cuenta
- [x] Pagué la cuenta
- Pago la cuenta

-gar verbs add a u in the yo form of the preterite so the g stays hard.

## How do you say "We ate two days ago"?

- Comemos hace dos días
- [x] Comimos hace dos días
- Comimos dos días hace

-er verbs change in the nosotros preterite (comemos → comimos), and "ago" is hace + the time, in front.

## Problem

Your colleague asks: "¿Qué hiciste ayer?" (What did you do yesterday?) Answer in four sentences, each with a different regular verb in the preterite. Include one -car, -gar or -zar verb in the yo form, and one thing you did with someone else (nosotros).

## Solution

One version:

> Ayer trabajé en casa por la mañana. Por la tarde busqué un regalo para mi madre. Llegué a casa a las siete. Después mi novia y yo cenamos y miramos una serie.

What to check in yours:

- Every yo verb ends in **-é** (-ar) or **-í** (-er, -ir), with the accent.
- The spelling change: **busqué**, **llegué**, **pagué**, **empecé**.
- **Cenamos**, **miramos**: the nosotros form looks like the present, and **ayer** makes it past.
- You'll meet **hiciste** in lesson 11: hacer is irregular in the past.
